# August: From Coverage to Confidence [2026-08-24]

In August, we focused on making μForge.io more useful at the points that move a product forward: choosing a part, building a working baseline, and validating the result. The goal is simple: spend less time assembling an answer from scattered resources.

### Clearer choices before the schematic

The [SF32 Product Selector](../../sf32-products/product-selector.md) and family pages help turn requirements such as memory, package, I/O, power, display, and audio into a practical shortlist. It does not replace a data sheet; it gives you a better place to start before committing assumptions to a schematic.

### More useful paths into implementation

The [Projects](../../projects/index.md) library now collects reproducible starting points for real product classes. Each project is intended to be more than a demo: a hardware and software baseline that you can inspect, adapt, and use to judge the work needed for your own design.

Hardware design guides, checklists, and the Learn section support the next steps. They cover board-level constraints, measurement-led power work, and runnable examples for audio, graphics, USB, Bluetooth, and low power.

### When the documentation is not enough

The new [contact page](../../about/contact.md) gives you a direct route to ask about a product decision, a documentation gap, or an integration problem. Your questions also help identify where μForge.io should become clearer next.

μForge.io is still early, but the path is taking shape: shortlist the right product, learn from a working baseline, validate the design, and get help when the details become difficult.
